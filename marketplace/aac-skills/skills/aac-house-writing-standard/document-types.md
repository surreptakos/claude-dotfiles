# Deliverable-specific rules

Companion to `SKILL.md`, which carries the rules every deliverable shares. Each section below adds the rules for one deliverable type.

## Email


- Subject names topic, project or customer, and the required action: "Clearbrook Northwind - Proposal Approval Needed." Never bare "Question," "Update," "FYI." Update the subject when a thread changes topic.
- Open with purpose. Greeting optional by relationship. Long or decision emails: bottom line first (what, who, by when), then explanation.
- Name the owner of each action. Several owners get separate lines: "Chris: Complete the estimate."
- Deadlines specific: "Friday, September 11, at 3 p.m.," not ASAP.
- To = people who act or are addressed; Cc = people who need visibility. Cc is not a way to assign work.
- Name necessary attachments and give them filenames that survive saving.
- Internal routine messages need no ceremonial closing; external formal messages close with Thank you, Regards, or Sincerely.
- Approved signature: name, title, company, phone. No quotations, images, decorative fonts, or extra colors.

## Teams and chat


- Assignment messages still name person, action, timing. @mention only people who must act. Never let Teams be the only record of a customer commitment, pricing approval, scope change, or policy decision; record it in the CRM, ticket, document, or email.

## Letters and memos


- Full block: everything flush left, no indents, colon after the salutation ("Dear Mr. Smith:"; "Dear Mark:" in a first-name relationship), comma after the complimentary close.
- Sequence: letterhead, date, recipient name and title, organization, street, city/state/ZIP, salutation, optional subject line, body, close, signature space, typed name, title, enclosure or attachment notation, copy notation. Do not use both "Subject:" and "Re:".
- Memo for anything that should become part of the formal record; email or Teams for routine coordination. Guide words DATE, TO, FROM, SUBJECT, entries aligned; full-block body; purpose in the first paragraph; headings once a memo passes a page.

## Reports


- Sections as the purpose requires: cover, executive summary, contents, background, findings, analysis, recommendations, actions, appendices, sources. Do not add sections for the look of it.
- Executive summary states the principal finding, the evidence, the recommended action, and the main risk. It does not describe the report.
- Table of contents once a document passes about five substantive pages, generated from heading styles.
- Every recurring management report states its reporting period or data-as-of date.
- Separate what the data says, why it happened, and what to do; never state an interpretation as a measured fact.

## Proposals and scopes of work


- Each scope item says who performs, what is furnished, what is installed or changed, how many, where, and under what assumption. "Active Alarm will furnish and install two card readers at the rear double doors," not "Add readers at rear doors."
- State quantities wherever omission could create a dispute. Specific locations; no "as needed" or "where required" unless another document defines them.
- Name the other party: "The electrical contractor will provide a dedicated 120 V circuit at the control panel," not "120 V by others." "By others" only when the party is undetermined or the contract format requires it.
- Exclusions and assumptions stated directly: "Price excludes 120 V power wiring." "Pricing assumes existing conduit is reusable."

## Procedures and SOPs


- Structure: title, document number, version, effective date, owner, purpose, scope, responsibilities, prerequisites, procedure, exceptions or escalation, records, related documents, revision history (unless the controlled SOP system dictates otherwise; the aac-sop skill governs the docx build).
- Numbered steps, each starting with a verb, one action per step, substeps for detail. Roles, not names. State who can authorize an exception. Screenshots only when they materially help, with no personal or customer data.

## Contracts and technical writing


- Never restyle approved contract language for house preferences. Capitalize a contract term only when defined. Exact legal entity names in contracts, notices, releases, certificates.
- Preserve manufacturer names, part numbers, model numbers, serial numbers, device IDs, drawing references, door numbers, software versions, code sections, and customer identifiers exactly. No punctuation added, no leading zeros stripped, no "correction." Copy identifiers from the source.
- Cite a code with enough precision to verify: standard, edition, section, jurisdiction. Do not say a code requires something until confirmed.
