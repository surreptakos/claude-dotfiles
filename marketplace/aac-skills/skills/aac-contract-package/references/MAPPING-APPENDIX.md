## How to use this appendix
Use this document together with the **Active Alarm Contract Package Review Living Standard — Final**.

This appendix governs how scheduled recurring services map into the master agreement selections and how common recurring service items should be described internally for pricing and review purposes. The schedule remains the commercial source of truth. The master agreement must follow the schedule and this appendix.

## 1) General mapping rules
1. Master agreement selections must follow the schedule, not the other way around.
2. A customer has one applicable master agreement. Scheduled services become part of that agreement upon signature — they are not "added to" or "landed on" a separate document. Reviewers do not ask which master governs; they confirm the selections on it match the schedule.
3. Billing frequency must be checked in the master agreement. Default is **Quarter Annual** unless the customer specifically requested something else.
4. Use approved service names only. **"Service Agreement"** is not valid; use **"Repair Service."**
5. Repair Service means parts and labor unless the clarifications expressly state a narrower coverage position.
6. If separate recurring services are sold separately, list each price in the appropriate boxes. Name the exact boxes per this appendix and the RMR Items sheet. **IN LIEU OF** is correct only on the **Other / See Schedule** route, where all RMR pricing combines on that line — never where services are itemized in their own boxes. When combining there, do not split the master agreement wording into fragmented unsupported entries. If the correct boxes cannot be identified from this appendix and the RMR Items sheet, select **IN LIEU OF** with the Monthly Total rather than guessing. [This is the one full statement of the IN LIEU OF rule (ratified 2026-08-19, wayfinder ticket #8). The Contract Package Rules' combine note, pre-send item 11, and the PROMPT.md email-instruction rule are point-of-use copies that cite it.]
7. If a mapping requires **Other / See Schedule**, make sure the schedule wording is explicit enough to support the master agreement description.
8. If the schedule shows existing services, only do so when a new master agreement is being sent.
9. If a recurring price is rule-based rather than fixed, the shipped figure still comes from the proposal; validate it per §3a rather than deriving it.
10. If a schedule item is not listed in this appendix, escalate rather than improvising a mapping.
11. The RMR service name must match the price. If the price on the schedule belongs to a different named tier in the table below, the name is wrong, the price is wrong, or both; sort out which before approval. Exception: where the schedule price matches the issued proposal, the rate was quoted upstream and is not the reviewer's to correct. Flag the mismatch to the rep and hold.

## 2) Required companion review checks
Before approving any schedule-to-master mapping, confirm:
- the service name on the schedule matches an approved item or an approved equivalent,
- the selected master agreement boxes align with the scheduled services,
- the quantity basis is shown where relevant,
- the recurring subtotal is present,
- the pricing aligns with the approved pricing source, or passed §3a validation,
- itemized services are not improperly collapsed into an **"In lieu of…"** structure,
- related addenda exist when Repair Service or Inspections are sold.

## 3a) Rule-based RMR pricing — validation only
Rewritten 2026-08-19 (ratified by Dan, wayfinder ticket #7). The derivation formulas this section once carried live in `ESTIMATING-APPENDIX.md`. The drafter and reviewer never derive a Repair Service or Inspection price.

- The shipped Repair Service or Inspection figure comes from the proposal, like every other price (SCHEDULE-GENERATION-PROCEDURE §1, rank 1).
- The FSI worksheet must be on file in the job folder's Source docs for any Repair Service RMR. Verify the shipped figure against it. A missing FSI, or a figure that does not reproduce from the worksheet, goes to the rep as a question — never a silent recomputation.
- Show the monthly figure and the quantity basis (per camera, per door, per site) on the schedule.
- How these prices are derived — the FSI method, the 1% rule and its basis, the greater-of rule, the inspection service-call formula — is estimating policy, recorded in `ESTIMATING-APPENDIX.md` with its open items.

### Repair Service start date
**Repair Service RMR bills from month 13**, the first month after the one-year parts-and-labor warranty ends (ruled by Dan, 2026-09-22, Premier Eye Care Z-4260 review; reverses the 2026-08-19 cutover ruling, OPEN-DECISIONS item 24). Commercial Security master ¶12 makes the warranty parts and labor on any defective part for one year; ¶7 Repair Service carries the same exclusion list (batteries, surges, lightning, software, unsupported communicators, obsolete parts). In year one Repair Service adds only labor on calls with no defect, which does not justify a year of billing. The earlier rationale — that Repair Service covers surges, lightning and user damage the warranty does not — was wrong against ¶7.

On the schedule: the Repair Service line carries its full monthly price with "(begins one year from installation completion date)" at the end of the description; the Monthly Total is the full-term figure; the SOW states both amounts, "The monthly amount is $X for one year following installation completion and $Y per month thereafter." On the master, the IN LIEU OF line or the 4(b)(ii) box carries the same two figures in that form. Cloud subscriptions and Remote Technical Support still bill from cutover (§4a). Wording is Dan's, from the Z-4260 schedule of 2026-09-22.

## 4a) Cloud subscriptions: bought up front, billed monthly
Cloud video and cloud access subscriptions — Alta Video, Alta Access, EEN, and the like — are purchased for a full year up front from the manufacturer and billed to the customer monthly over that year. Both entries belong on the deal at the same time: the annual license cost sits inside the installation price, and the monthly RMR line runs from cutover.

This is intentional and it is not a double charge. Three reasons:

1. **Cash flow.** We pay the manufacturer for twelve months on day one. Recovering that in the install price keeps us whole immediately instead of fronting the money.
2. **Renewal funding.** Monthly billing across the year means the next annual renewal is paid out of the customer's money rather than ours.
3. **We are selling more than the license.** The monthly charge covers platform access and our tech support, not just the manufacturer's subscription.

Reviewers should not question a first-year subscription appearing in both the install price and the monthly services. Both are correct.

## 4) Cross-cutting pricing and packaging notes
- Residential discount language applies only when the residential customer is paying the same standard rate as the comparable commercial service and company policy allows the discount. The discount must appear **exactly once** across the WU, the schedule, and the master agreement — a discount applied on both the WU and the schedule under-prices the job.
- Where the note requires a quote from Honeywell, a supplier, Openpath, or another approved source, the quote must be obtained before the package is approved.
- Where the schedule item is priced **per door**, **per camera**, or by another quantity basis, that basis must appear on the schedule.
- Repair Service and Inspections require covered-equipment support. If the same equipment list applies to both, say so.
- If services are itemized separately on the schedule, do not collapse them into **"In lieu of…"** language on the master agreement.
- Use the approved service label on the agreement; do not invent new labels.
- Subcontracted mechanical/lockwork costs (door hardware subs, door closer subs, etc.) roll into the AAC installation price and are not broken out as separate line items on the customer-facing schedule. A reviewer should not flag the absence of a lockwork line item as a missing equipment entry.

## 5) Escalation triggers for mapping issues
Escalate before approval if:
- the schedule line item does not clearly map to a master agreement selection,
- the schedule uses a non-standard service label,
- the price does not match the approved pricing source, or does not reproduce from the FSI on file,
- the RMR service name belongs to a different price tier than the price shown,
- the item requires **Other / See Schedule** but the schedule wording is vague,
- the item is bundled on the schedule even though the master agreement expects it to be split,
- a required lease, FSI support, or covered-equipment addendum is missing.
