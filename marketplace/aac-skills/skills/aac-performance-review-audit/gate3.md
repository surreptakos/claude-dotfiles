# Gate 3: Consistency (judgment)

Only after Gates 1 and 2 pass. The coaching email, and the only gate that goes to data or debates the Rating.

Figures: pull every figure off the page. Sort into verifiable from a connector (Zoho CRM deals and Lead Source, Zoho Desk tickets, Zoho Analytics), verifiable from a file Dan uploads (commission workbooks, comp plan, prior review), verifiable only by the manager. Verify the first bucket. Ask Dan for the second once, in one batch, naming the files. The third goes to the manager as "please confirm." Report under the Notes line as figure / source / matched, off by X, or unverified. Only mismatches reach the manager, as questions. Figures pulled to a different cutoff than the header are recomputed, not called wrong.

Behavior claims are taken at face value; the manager owns them.

Also: prior review repeats and prior Guidance carried or closed; Rating against evidence (sales roles: the signed comp plan's quotas, New Logo and Self-Sourced definitions and mix gates) with the counter-case named; Rating, Result and Ramification consistent with each other and with the preponderance of the page (not every item must support the Core Message); Guidance covers the Ramification as well as the Weaknesses; self-appraisal reconciled (every item the direct raised is in the review or consciously left out); prose in the manager's voice, AI cadence flagged once under the Notes line and never as an accusation.

Coaching email voice: Dan asks questions of a manager who knows the direct better than he does. Format rules are stated plainly. Judgments are the manager's first: agree out loud ("I agree that Met Expectations is the right rating"), push back as a question ("What put you at Met rather than Exceeded? He beat both quotas."), never nominate an example the manager did not supply, never invent a date, number, target, or name. Shape: "Hey [Name]," opener with two or three specifics that are good and the two or three things to look at; "Core message" bullets (Rating, Result, Ramification, Wording); "Strengths" with one SEER model and one Sum-Ex model then one line per remaining item; "Weaknesses" with one model; "Guidance" molded from the manager's own points, naming which lack a measure (the measure is his to set); closing instruction to read every sentence and count them; "Thanks," and nothing after. Under 1,100 words. No tables, no options labeled A/B/C, no explanation of the audit's reasoning in the email.

A review that passes Gate 3 ships. Say so in one line and stop editing.
