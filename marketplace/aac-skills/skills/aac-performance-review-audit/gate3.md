# Gate 3: Consistency (judgment)

Only after Gates 1 and 2 pass. The coaching email, and the only gate that goes to data or debates the Rating.

Figures: pull every figure off the page. Sort into verifiable from a connector (Zoho CRM deals and Lead Source, Zoho Desk tickets, Zoho Analytics), verifiable from a file Dan uploads (commission workbooks, comp plan, prior review), verifiable only by the manager. Verify the first bucket. Ask Dan for the second once, in one batch, naming the files. The third goes to the manager as "please confirm." Report under the Notes line as figure / source / matched, off by X, or unverified. Only mismatches reach the manager, as questions. Figures pulled to a different cutoff than the header are recomputed, not called wrong. This reaches a figure whose range stops short of the period ("closed 136 deals from 7/24/25 through 6/30/26" against a 7/23/26 header). It does not reach a status stated with its data-as-of date ("neither plan exists in written form as of 6/30/26"): that labels when the data was pulled, which is correct practice, and makes no claim about where the period ends. Leave those alone.

Behavior claims are taken at face value; the manager owns them.

No surprises: every Weakness is something the direct has already heard from the manager this year (Manager Tools, No Surprises In Reviews). A repeat from the prior review passes. For any other Weakness, ask whether the direct has heard it before, in a One on One or at the time; take the answer at face value. Not heard: it comes off the page, may be said in the meeting, and does not weigh on the Rating.

Discipline at stake: if anything in the file (prior review, write-ups, warnings, the self-appraisal, what Dan knows) shows a behavior where no improvement could lead to discipline, it is on the page as a Weakness with a Guidance point covering it (Manager Tools, OnePage section 7). Gate 2 checks that every Weakness has a Guidance point; this checks that the behavior made it onto the page at all. Ask it as a question ("The write-up from March is not in the review. Did you leave it out on purpose?"), and note under the Notes line what the record shows. Nothing in the file: nothing to raise. A behavior the direct has never been told about is the exception: it follows the no-surprises rule above and stays off the page.

Written moderate, spoken full strength: the page is a little cooler than the meeting in both directions, praise and criticism (Dan, 9/23/26). Do not push a manager to write a Strength more glowingly or a Weakness more harshly; do flag a Rating, figure or example the evidence does not support, in either direction. The Rating never softens, because the raise follows it. When a review points toward termination within the year without improvement (Did Not Meet Expectations with a repeat Weakness, or a discipline item), ask whether he holds the One on One and coaching notes behind each Weakness before it is delivered (Manager Tools, Shot Across The Bow Review). AAC does not write a second, harsher review.

Also: prior review repeats and prior Guidance carried or closed; Rating against evidence (sales roles: the signed comp plan's quotas, New Logo and Self-Sourced definitions and mix gates) with the counter-case named; Rating, Result and Ramification consistent with each other and with the preponderance of the page (not every item must support the Core Message); self-appraisal reconciled (every item the direct raised is in the review or consciously left out); prose in the manager's voice, AI cadence flagged once under the Notes line and never as an accusation.

Coaching email voice: Dan asks questions of a manager who knows the direct better than he does. Format rules are stated plainly. Judgments are the manager's first: agree out loud in one line with no reasoning behind it ("Rating: I agree with Met Expectations."; Dan, 9/24/26: explain only where you disagree), push back as a question ("What put you at Met rather than Exceeded? He beat both quotas."), never nominate an example the manager did not supply, never invent a date, number, target, or name. Shape: "Hey [Name]," opener with two or three specifics that are good and the two or three things to look at; "Core message" bullets (Rating, Result, Ramification, Wording); "Strengths" with one SEER model and one Sum-Ex model then one line per remaining item; "Weaknesses" with one model; "Guidance" molded from the manager's own points, with no request for measures or details, which Manager Tools leaves to the weeks after the review; closing instruction to read every sentence and count them; "Thanks," and nothing after. Under 1,100 words. No tables, no options labeled A/B/C, no explanation of the audit's reasoning in the email.

Before it goes out, the email passes the release gate in SKILL.md (house writing standard, linter exit 0, Rule 166 confirmed). A review that passes Gate 3 ships. Say so in one line and stop editing.

## Findings carried in from Gate 2

When the self-check stamp was valid, Gate 2 did not run as its own round and its findings arrive here. Put them in the coaching email as part of the page-level conversation, not as a numbered fix list: name the item, say what the sentence does now, and leave the wording to him. They rank below the figures and the Rating, which are what Gate 3 is for.
